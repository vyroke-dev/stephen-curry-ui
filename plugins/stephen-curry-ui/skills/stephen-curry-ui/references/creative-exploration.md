# Creative direction without formulaic output

Use exploration when the direction is open and comparing options can improve the decision. A focused refinement or user-selected concept does not need three new directions.

1. Inspect the current repository, brand, content, design rules, assets, rights, and relevant prior decisions.
2. For new or broad redesign work, research whole-site references across relevant routes, sections, mobile navigation, and important states. Label live observations, inference, and unknowns.
3. State an earned creative thesis connecting the subject's actual material, product, process, or culture to the audience's experience.
4. When the project has room for creative direction, produce at least three substantially different concepts. Vary composition, journey, interaction, media behavior, mobile treatment, and ending—not just palette or type.
5. Use the local explorer for comparison when helpful. It is not a final interactive prototype. Record rejected directions only when that helps preserve a consequential decision.
6. Ask for approval only for meaningful ambiguity or a required checkpoint; otherwise choose a direction within the user's authority and explain the rationale.

Base visual choices on real content and user goals. Avoid generic patterns by reflex, but do not ban a style categorically when it fits the project.

For substantial work, useful artifacts may include a reference dossier, a direction set, an explorer preview, and a project-local `DESIGN.md`. Make only the ones the decision needs.

An ambitious website should have at least one deliberately authored experience moment. Prototype and verify it where interaction risk warrants. Not every business site needs 3D; every site needs a clear point of view.
